Project Overview

A comprehensive UX research study designed to understand the needs, frustrations, and behaviors of patients using digital healthcare tools. The research informed the redesign of a patient-facing application used by thousands of users.

Problem

The healthcare application had low engagement and high drop-off. Users found it confusing, intimidating, and hard to navigate — especially older patients.

  • Low daily active users despite large patient base
  • High abandonment during onboarding
  • Confusing medical terminology
  • Poor accessibility for older adults

Research Methods

A mixed-methods approach combining qualitative and quantitative research — ensuring both depth and scale.

Interviews

40 in-depth interviews with diverse patients

Surveys

120 responses on digital health habits

Usability Tests

15 participants tested 3 competitor apps

Analytics

Funnel analysis of 50K+ sessions

Research Questions

  • Why do patients abandon the app during onboarding?
  • What level of medical terminology is appropriate?
  • How do older adults interact with mobile health tools?
  • What builds trust in a digital health experience?

Key Insights

Five insights emerged from the research — each directly shaping the redesign of the application.

Simple Language

Patients want clarity, not medical jargon

Trust & Transparency

Calm visual design builds digital trust

Accessibility First

Many users are older — accessibility is required

Family Sharing

Accounts must support family involvement

Impact

+45%Onboarding Completion
+60%Daily Engagement

Lessons Learned

  • Research must include the quiet voices
  • Accessibility is a research finding, not an afterthought
  • Trust is built through clarity
  • Insights need to be actionable to matter
